# Claims sheet

Every number that may be said on stage, in the pitch deck or to a judge. If a
number is not on this sheet, do not say it. If a number is on this sheet, say
it with its condition.

Numbers come from `eval/results.md` (regenerated by `python -m eval.report`)
unless the source column names another file. The `§` column is the section
there.

**Conditions:**
* **simulated**: generator data (`generator/`, the origination corpus). It is a
  statement about our simulator, never about Bitcoin.
* **fixture**: a hand-built test fixture.
* **measured**: a property of this build on this machine, not of a model.

## Quotable findings

Ready to say as written. Each sentence carries its own condition.

1. **The zero-ceiling result.** "With a single observer, a broadcaster two hops
   away never appears among the candidates, so no estimator can name it. The
   ceiling is zero, not low. So we report adjacent and non-adjacent captures
   separately, never pooled. When every peer that announced a transaction is a
   public relay, the system abstains with the reason NOT_REACHABLE rather than
   naming a relay. On our simulated corpus, the origin is among the candidates
   for only 28% of transactions." (docs/GROUND_TRUTH.md §1; §9, O5)

2. **The tie-break bug.** "Every propagation tree ties at its earliest
   timestamp, because one relay record stamps both ends of a hop. Our first
   estimator broke that tie by dictionary insertion order, which happened to
   favour the true origin. It inflated published top-1 by about fourteen points
   whenever the row order changed. A test that reshuffles the input rows caught
   it. The tie is now broken on the evidence, which end of the hop was sending,
   and the published numbers moved by 0.002." (commit 24ff552;
   `tests/test_propagation.py::test_an_estimator_is_a_function_of_the_evidence_not_the_row_order`;
   simulated)

3. **Tiered validity.** "Enforced as a blanket gate, our validity layer made
   the score worse, 0.057 down to 0.034, because it withheld right answers. So
   we changed what an answer may claim instead of whether to give one. A
   CoinJoin answer names the broadcaster and states that the inputs are not
   attributable. On the metric we pre-registered for that, it scores 0.053
   against 0.028 with no layer. It ties on the old metric, and it costs about
   4% in runtime." (§10, V1, V2; simulated / measured)

4. **The fingerprint turned itself off.** "Scored on the wallet profiles it was
   trained on, our fingerprint looks excellent. On a profile it had never seen,
   it gave a contradicting label 80% of the time. A novelty check cut that to
   34%. That is still above the 10% bar we registered before running it, so
   under our own rule the console does not show fingerprints." (§12, F1;
   simulated)

5. **The actor collapse.** "Our first draft of actor joining chained 357
   clusters and 217 peers into one component, through shared infrastructure. We
   restricted joins to residential or onion peers backed by an origination
   claim, and fixed that rule before the evaluation. Even so, across ten
   simulated datasets, the actor queue improved triage on no measure, and it
   wrongly merged 8 to 10% of alerted multi-cluster actors. The entity queue
   stays the default." (docs/ACTORS.md; §13, A1, A2)

6. **Exit points on deeper layering.** "On deeper, shifted layering, the trace
   reaches the true cash-out only 37% of the time under haircut taint and 49%
   under poison. We found out why: the missed sinks sit 7 to 11 hops from the
   source, either past the 8-hop limit or under the 0.1% haircut floor after
   repeated fan-outs. We did not retune the cut-offs on the evaluation." (§16,
   X1; simulated, upper bound)

## Known limits

Say these before a judge finds them.

* **Simulated data throughout.** Every model number measures our generator and
  our simulator, not Bitcoin. The simulator omits Poisson INV trickling, clock
  skew between observers, peer churn, wtxid relay and real GeoIP (§9 lists all
  of it).
* **No real signet capture yet.** The adjacent and non-adjacent signet rows in
  §9 are PENDING. The only committed captures are synthetic test fixtures.
* **VPN and cloud addresses that are not on a list count as residential.** IP
  class comes from the relay list, the Tor exit list and the hosting-ASN list.
  An address outside all three is `residential_or_unknown`, and without the
  GeoLite2 databases no ASN is known either.
* **No decoy exchanges.** The generator never routes illicit money past an
  exchange before the cash-out, so exit-point ranking is untested against
  services that should not be named (§16).
* **The OFAC parser is unverified against a real SDN.XML.** It was written from
  the published format and a hand-built fixture (`intel/importers.py`,
  docs/TAGSTORE.md). Check it against a downloaded copy before operational use.
* **Tiny denominators.** The canonical datasets hold 5 and 4 illicit
  operations. §13's seed sweep (about 30 each) exists because of that.
* **No GNN model is trained in this build.** `gnn_score` is 0 everywhere
  (report summary).
* **No authentication.** The custody ledger records what was done, not who did
  it.
